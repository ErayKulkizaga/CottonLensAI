# T55 preregistration: the report-day market test in the era when the revision signal worked (2000–2009)

Written 17 Nov 2026, before any 2000–2009 cotton price or return was loaded. Only the coverage of the price series was checked: Yahoo `CT=F` daily closes, 3,105 trading days from 2000-01-03 to 2009-12-31. No price value was printed.

## Why
- T52 (2010–2021) was null (p = 0.30). Our price history starts in 2010, so T52 could only cover the era in which the revision-direction signal no longer works (47% in its events).
- T50 found the signal works on blind cotton seasons up to 2011 (67%, p = 0.012).
- `CT=F` (the same front-month proxy as T52) has daily closes from 2000, which covers the remaining blind seasons.

## Design (identical to T52 except the period and the price source)
- **Prices:** Yahoo Finance chart API, symbol `CT=F`, daily close, 2000-01-01 to 2009-12-31. The raw response is saved with its SHA-256 before use. Nothing after 2009-12-31 is used.
- **Report days:** release dates parsed from the Crop Production reports (T45 forecasts, `release` field).
- **Signal, known one report earlier:** at report m−1, the sign of USDA's latest US production revision (m−2 → m−1). The prediction for report m is the same sign.
- **Events:** the Oct, Nov and Dec reports of 2000–2009. An event is skipped if the previous revision is zero or the release day has no close.
- **Trade direction:** −sign(signal). An expected downward revision means less cotton, so a higher price.
- **Return:** log close-to-close return on the report day.

## Pass rule
- **PASS:** the mean of −sign(signal) × return over all events is > 0, with one-sided season-block permutation p < 0.05 (all events of a season flipped together, 20,000 draws, seed 42).
- **Also reported:** the hit rate; the signal's own hit rate on USDA's actual revision in these events; the pooled 2000–2011 result with T52's 2010–2011 events, as a secondary view only.

## Prior expectation, stated before running
- About 30 events in 10 seasons, so power is low.
- Even if USDA's revisions are predictable, the market may already expect them: traders see the same reports and the same condition data. A null is the most likely outcome.
- The test is run once and reported either way. No other period, contract or return window will be tried if it fails.
